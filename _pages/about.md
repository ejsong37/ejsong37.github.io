I am a masters student at the University of California, San Diego (UCSD). My research interests are broadly probabilistic methods, deep generative models, causal inference and etc and their applications in computational biology and decision making. More specifically, I am interested in the following problems:


1. Theoretical Robustness: I seek to resolve fundamental issues currently limiting the impact of deep generative models, focusing on developing novel methods to ensure they are tractable, scalable, and inherently causal. This includes research into efficient inference mechanisms and frameworks that explicitly model causal relationships, which is vital for reliable decision-making.
2. Scientific Application: I am motivated to apply these robust probabilistic frameworks to challenging, real-world problems. My interests lie in applying these models to computational biology, where accurate probabilistic inference is essential, and to decision-making under uncertainty, including problems in social learning and algorithmic mechanism design (e.g., multi-armed bandits).

I did my undergrad also at the UCSD, where I was also worked at the Scripps Research Instute advised by Prof. Pejman Mohammadi (now at the University of Washington). I am also grateful to have worked with Prof. Yuhua Zhu for my undergrad capstone.

In my free time, I enjoy playing billiards, reading, and cooking.
